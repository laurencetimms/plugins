# Charts: This Week in…

## Work out the week

- A date ("2 November 1983") → the UK chart week containing it.
- "This week in 1984" → today's day and month in that year.
- "My birthday" or another personal date → ask for the date if you don't have it.
- Default chart: the Official UK Singles Chart. If the user asks for the US, use Billboard's Hot 100 and say so. Other countries: say which chart you used.
- Default range is any year, but the plugin's heart is roughly 1977–1989.

## Get the chart

1. The Official Charts archive has a page per week: `https://www.officialcharts.com/charts/singles-chart/YYYYMMDD/7501/`. Any date inside the week works; the page shows the week's start and end dates.
2. If fetching that address directly is refused, search for "officialcharts.com singles chart" with the date and open the result.
3. Read positions, artist, title, last week's position and weeks on chart from the page. Never fill gaps from memory. If the page is cut short, say how far it goes.

## Present it

Lead with the top 10 as a compact table (position, artist, title, movement). Then:

- **The story of the week.** Two or three short paragraphs: what was rising fast, what was falling, any big new entry, anything odd (novelty records, comebacks, a song that later became huge but was stuck at 47 that week). Base it on the chart's own numbers; add context from sources you open.
- **Synth spotting.** Point out the electronic records in the top 40, and offer forensics on any of them in one line, e.g. "Want to know what made the Too Shy bass?".
- **Offer the rest.** Positions 11–40 or 11–75 on request, not by default.

Keep it to what a curious friend would enjoy, not an almanac.

## The playlist recommender

When the user wants a playlist from the week, build three short sections:

1. **The hits:** 6–8 of the week's best or most characteristic records, not just the top 8.
2. **Lower down:** 3–4 good records outside the top 20 that week, or new entries that went on to matter.
3. **Same sound, not in the chart:** 3–4 records from the same few months that shared the week's sound or machines, from the gear graph where possible, marked with why.

Give one line per track on why it's there. Then hand over following `references/playlists.md`.
