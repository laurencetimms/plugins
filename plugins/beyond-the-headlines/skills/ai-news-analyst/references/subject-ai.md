# Subject: AI

What the analyst needs to know about AI news specifically. Use it alongside `method.md`.

## Claim types that recur in AI news

| Claim | What it often turns out to be | Questions to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "AI solved / discovered X" | Rediscovery of a published answer; an obscure problem rather than a hard one; a genuine milestone; or a contested claim on a narrower version of the problem | Was it already in the literature? Is it the famous version or a variant? Who has checked it? |
| "Model beats humans at X" | A benchmark result, not real-world performance | What does the benchmark measure? Has the test leaked into training data? How does it do on the messy version of the task? |
| "Company replaces N jobs with AI" | Cuts driven by cost, interest rates or restructuring, with AI as the stated reason, or the reverse | What did filings and earnings calls say? Gross or net? Layoffs or posts not refilled? Over what period? |
| "AI boosts productivity by N%" | A vendor or consultancy study, self-reported, on narrow tasks | Who funded it? Was it measured or surveyed? On which tasks, for which workers? |
| "N% of firms now use AI" | A survey with a loose definition of "use" | What counts as use? Who was sampled? Compare with official statistics where they exist |
| "Government regulates AI" | An announcement, consultation, voluntary commitment, or law not yet in force | Announced, enacted or enforced? Binding or voluntary? What are the penalties? |
| "$N billion for AI infrastructure" | Commitments and options, not money spent | Spent or pledged? Over how many years? Who is financing it? |
| "AI system did something alarming" | A test under deliberately unusual conditions, or a real-world incident | Test or deployment? Which safeguards were on? Who ran it and published it? |

## The incentive map

- **AI labs:** showing capability attracts customers, talent and investment, and shapes regulation. Their research results are real but selectively presented.
- **Investors and analysts:** valuations depend on the story. Bullish and bearish forecasts both serve positions.
- **Vendors and consultancies:** adoption and productivity studies are often marketing.
- **Governments:** industrial strategy, national security and competition with other states shape what they announce and how.
- **Critics and campaign groups:** vital for scrutiny, but they also need attention and funding.
- **Researchers:** many receive funding, compute or employment from labs. Note the affiliation.
- **Media:** "AI does X" travels further than "AI does X under conditions Y".
- **The analyst's own maker:** Anthropic is a major AI lab. Disclose whenever Anthropic or Claude is involved in a story.

## Where verification lives

- Peer review, independent replication, and responses from other researchers to preprints.
- Formal verification (for example, proofs checked in Lean) for mathematics and software. This checks the proof, not whether the formal statement matches the original question.
- Official labour statistics, such as the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, the UK Office for National Statistics and Eurostat, rather than layoff trackers alone.
- Statutory texts, regulators' published decisions, and court filings.
- Independent evaluators, such as government AI safety and security institutes.
- Company filings and earnings calls, where executives speak under legal obligations.
- Procurement records and audit offices for public-sector use.

## Recurring traps

- **Press releases passed through aggregators.** Ten articles from one announcement is one source.
- **"First" and "unprecedented".** These are often untrue, and usually unchecked.
- **Generalising from domains that can be checked.** Maths and code give AI a way to know when it is right. Messier fields like policy, medicine and management don't, so success in one says less about the others.
- **Gross versus net job effects, and timing.** Displacement can stay small in good times and accelerate in downturns. A quiet labour market now is not evidence of no effect.
- **Targets reported as outcomes.** "Will cut 25,000 jobs" is often "estimates 25,000 fewer posts by a date, partly by not refilling them".
- **US-centric coverage.** Europe, China, India, Africa and Latin America are under-covered, or seen only through a Western lens.
- **Treating announcements as history.** Revisit past claims: the quiet retraction is a story too.

## Zones that tend to be under-covered

- AI in public services: courts, policing, welfare, councils, schools. This is where many people actually meet AI, and outcomes are often unmeasured.
- The hidden workforce: data labelling, content moderation and evaluation work, often offshore.
- Physical costs: energy, water, grid capacity, local planning disputes.
- How people become experts: junior roles, apprenticeships and training pipelines, as entry-level tasks are automated.
- Small businesses and non-tech sectors.
- Accessibility and disability, both gains and harms.
- Creative work and the contracts governing it.
- Democratic processes: consultations, elections, public comment flooded by automated content.

## Worked example: "AI is solving maths problems humans couldn't"

This shows the method in action; the specifics will date. Under one headline sat four distinct things:

1. **Rediscovery.** Problems marked "open" on a database turned out to have published solutions that AI found by searching the literature.
2. **Obscure, not hard.** Problems were open because few people had looked at them, not because they were difficult.
3. **Genuine milestones.** A small number of results that leading mathematicians accepted as new and significant.
4. **Contested frontier claims.** Results on a narrower variant of a famous problem, not accepted by the relevant prize body, with disputes over credit and data provenance.

Beneath that, the people closest to it were arguing about different things: who would check a flood of AI-written proofs, whether solving problems was ever the real point of mathematics, who gets credit, and what happens to how young mathematicians learn. That is the story the headline missed.
